Research

Neural control of homeostasis

How the brain interprets visceral and vestibular information to coordinate autonomic, metabolic, behavioral, and immune responses.

01

Gut–brain and vagal sensory signaling

I investigate how gastrointestinal signals are detected by vagal sensory pathways and relayed to the brain. A major focus is on gut-derived hormones such as GLP-1 and CCK and how these signals engage hypothalamic circuits to regulate feeding, autonomic output, and metabolism.

02

Thermoregulation and energy metabolism

My work examines neural pathways controlling body temperature and brown adipose tissue thermogenesis. Current projects connect peripheral hormonal signals with hypothalamic oxytocin and vasopressin systems and sympathetic control of thermogenic organs.

03

Vestibular physiology and altered gravity

I study how vestibular circuits adapt to prolonged or repeated changes in gravitational load. The goal is to understand the neural basis of vestibular compensation, resilience, and autonomic adaptation relevant to both terrestrial physiology and spaceflight.

04

Neuroimmune regulation

I am interested in how autonomic sensory pathways alter inflammatory responses in peripheral organs. This includes vagal afferent signaling, central autonomic circuits, sympathetic output, and the spleen–lung axis during acute inflammation.
